# GLead pilot kit

For the four-week pilot in `research/R5-measurement.md` §4. Two audiences: the participant (§1–§3) and the person running the pilot (§4–§5).

## 1. Before you start (participant, 15 minutes)

1. Open the GLead link in **Chrome on a laptop** or **Safari on an iPad or iPhone**. Add it to your home screen if offered (Share → Add to Home Screen on iOS; the install icon in Chrome). It works offline afterwards.
2. **Settings → Answer on the guitar (microphone): On.** Then **Test the microphone**: play a few single notes and watch the line under the button. It should name the note you played at 80% or more. If it names the wrong note more than once in ten, write that down and tell us; you can keep going with the mic off and answer by tapping.
3. Settings also has **Degree labels** (1 2 3 or do re mi) and a left-handed neck. The app has already chosen a label scheme for you; change it only if you cannot work with it, and tell us if you do.
4. **Today → Take the placement test** (about six minutes). It only measures; nothing is taught, and a wrong answer costs nothing.

## 2. Every day (10 minutes, five days a week)

1. Open **Today**. Leave the skills it has selected unless one says *(resting)*: that skill is waiting for a retest and must not be practised.
2. Choose **10 min** and **Start session**. Read the goal, tap **Begin**.
3. Answer on the guitar where you can: play the note you heard. When the app is unsure what you played it asks you to confirm; answer honestly. Tap the degree buttons only when you cannot play.
4. After the trials, take the **play-along** when it is offered: a loop plays and you land on the chord tone it names at each change.
5. Write one line in the reflection box: what was hard, what you will try next time.
6. If **Today** shows **Retest due**, do the retest first that day. It is in keys you have not practised and only measures.

## 3. Every week (2 minutes)

**Progress → Export my data (JSON)** and send the file. It holds which exercises you answered, in what key, what you answered, whether it was right, how long it took and how sure the microphone was. It never holds audio.

## 4. Running the pilot (the author)

- **Day 0 checklist per participant:** device and browser noted; mic test result noted (what it said for an open E, A and D); placement taken; experiment arms copied from Settings ("Your experiment arms").
- **Weekly:** open **Progress → View an export file** and load each participant's file. The dashboard shows their delayed retests, transfer, chord-tone hit rate, per-skill report and experiment arms exactly as it shows your own. Select several files at once for the cohort view: one row per participant, and the experiment arms pooled with trial and participant counts. Nothing is saved on your device. Note anything odd: a skill with many trials and no criterion, a confirm share above 20%, retests not run on the due day.
- **Stopping rule:** a participant who reports the mic accepting wrong notes or rejecting right ones more than a few times a session switches to tap mode. Record it: that is the R2 finding.
- **End:** the 20-minute call (R5 §4 step 5).

## 5. What to report

Per participant: the per-skill table (baseline, trials, recent, trials to criterion, learning rate, 1d/7d/30d, transfer), the chord-tone hit rate over the four weeks, confirm share, and the experiment table. Then the within-person comparisons with trial counts beside every percentage, and the plain statement that n is small.
