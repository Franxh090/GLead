# Teacher interview guide

Thirty minutes with a working guitar teacher who teaches intermediate players, on the curriculum sketch in `docs/prd-research-appendix.md` §12 and the drills as built. The aim is to find what the sketch gets wrong, not to sell it. Three to five interviews (roadmap, Sprints 5–6).

## Before the call

Send the one-paragraph description of GLead and the six-level curriculum table. Ask them to have a guitar to hand.

## 1. The plateau (8 minutes)

1. Think of your last three students who got stuck after the basics. What could they do, and what could they not?
2. When one of them hears a chord progression, what do they hear? What do they miss?
3. How do you get a student from "knows the shapes" to "hears where the song is going"? What is the first thing you make them do?
4. What do you make them do before they improvise over changes? Chord tones first, scale first, something else?

## 2. The sequence (10 minutes)

Show the six levels. For each boundary, ask:

5. Level 1 → 2: is "tonic vs not, then I IV V" the right first hearing, or would you start somewhere else?
6. Level 3: 3rds before 5ths and 7ths. Agree? What about roots and 5ths together first?
7. Where do minor keys enter for your students: alongside major from the start, or later?
8. Which level do your students usually stall at, and why?
9. Anything in the list you would drop? Anything missing that you teach every stuck intermediate?

## 3. The drills (8 minutes)

Demonstrate B1 (cadence, one note, play it back) and B3 (chord, one note, which tone), then a Changes take.

10. Is playing the note back on the guitar the right answer, or would you have them sing it first?
11. The key changes every trial. Would your students stand that? Would you?
12. Do the degree labels matter to you: numbers or do re mi? Why?
13. The play-along judges the first note after each change. Is "land on a chord tone at the change" how you would score it?

## 4. Close (4 minutes)

14. If you could put one exercise in front of every stuck intermediate for ten minutes a day, what would it be?
15. Would you let a student of yours try this for four weeks? What would you want to see at the end?

## Afterwards

Write up within a day: three things the teacher confirmed, three things they would change, one surprise. Put each proposed change against a curriculum level or a PRD ID, and note whether it needs a new drill or a parameter change. Decisions go in `docs/implementation-plan.md` §5.
